# Build your story bank: STAR(L) structure and the eight stories every senior engineer needs
**Round:** 15 · Leadership & Behavioral · **Level:** Senior · **Time:** 15 min · **Tags:** behavioral · star · storytelling
Source: https://whetstone-v1.vercel.app/#leadership-behavioral

Scenario: final loop in two weeks, stories are vague. Build a bank that covers most behavioral prompts.

## What they're really testing
Preparation and self-awareness: can you tell a specific, quantified, first-person story in about two minutes, at the scope your target level expects.

## Core answer (key points)
- **6-8 real stories,** each rehearsed in STAR+L (Situation, Task, Action, Result, Learning), about two minutes.
- **"I", not "we":** your decisions front and center, while crediting the team honestly.
- **Numbers in every Result:** time, money, latency, error rate, team size, screens/services.
- **Map each story to several prompts** so a small bank covers a big question space.
- **Scope rises with level:** Senior = owned a hard technical outcome. Lead/Staff = multiplied others, cross-team influence. Principal = set direction for an org.
- **The eight to have:**
  | Story | Answers prompts like |
  |---|---|
  | Large project driven end to end | most impactful work, ownership, complexity |
  | Migration/modernization | legacy, long-term thinking, risk |
  | Perf or reliability win with numbers | data-driven, impact |
  | Disagreement handled well | conflict, pushback, influence |
  | Failure/incident owned | mistake, learning |
  | Mentoring someone | leadership, raising the bar |
  | Trade-off under deadline | prioritization, ambiguity, scope cuts |
  | Cross-team alignment | influence without authority, stakeholders |

## Mental model / flow
Time budget for a ~2 min answer:
```
S 15s  context + stakes
T 10s  your responsibility
A 60-75s  3-4 decisions, why, alternatives rejected
R 15s  numbers + second-order effects
L 10s  what you'd do differently / now do by default
```

## STAR skeleton
Fill one of these per story; keep the filled bank in a private doc.
- **Situation:** What was the product/system? What was broken or at stake, in one measurable phrase? Why did it matter now?
- **Task:** What exactly were *you* accountable for? Who asked, and what did success look like?
- **Action:** Decision 1 (what, why, alternative rejected). Decision 2. Decision 3. The hard part: which constraint, skeptic or risk, and how you handled it.
- **Result:** Before -> after number. Second-order effect (team velocity, fewer pages, hiring, reuse). How you measured it.
- **Learning:** One thing you'd change; one habit you now apply by default.
- **Prompt map:** list 3-4 questions this story answers.

## Trade-offs & pitfalls
- "We" all the way through hides your role; all "I" sounds like you ignored the team. Balance.
- Too much Situation, too little Action. Action is where the signal is.
- No numbers; or invented numbers. Use approximate real ones and say "roughly".
- Same story for every question; interviewers notice.
- Stories below your target level (e.g. only individual coding wins for a staff loop).

## Likely follow-ups
- **"What would you do differently?"** Have a genuine answer ready per story.
- **"How did you measure it?"** Name the dashboard/metric and baseline.
- **"What did others think?"** Name the skeptic and how their view changed or didn't.
- **"What was your specific contribution?"** Separate your decisions from the team's execution.

## Say it in 60 seconds
I keep a bank of six to eight real stories: a large project, a migration, a measured perf or reliability win, a disagreement, a failure, mentoring, a deadline trade-off, and cross-team alignment. Each is rehearsed in STAR plus Learning in about two minutes, with most of the time on the three or four decisions I made and the alternatives I rejected, and a result with real numbers. I map each story to several questions so a small bank covers most prompts, and I pick stories whose scope matches the level: ownership for senior, multiplying others for staff, setting direction for principal.
